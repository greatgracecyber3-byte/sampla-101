Safeguarding Policy

Last updated: to be confirmed at publication · Draft pending review by counsel

This is a draft. It reflects the nature of the Foundation's work with women, widows, youth and students, but has not yet been reviewed by a safeguarding specialist or formally adopted by the Foundation's leadership.

1. Our commitment

External Excellencies Global Foundation works directly with women, widows, youth, and students through medical outreach, water projects, education support, and economic-empowerment programs. Because this work brings us into contact with people in vulnerable circumstances, we are committed to protecting the dignity, safety, and wellbeing of every beneficiary, staff member, volunteer, and partner we work with — especially children and other vulnerable groups.

2. Who this policy protects

This policy applies to everyone who takes part in or is affected by our programs, including: children and youth in our education and youth-empowerment initiatives; widows and women in our economic-empowerment and seed-capital programs; patients attending our medical camps and screening events; and staff, volunteers, and partner-organization representatives working alongside us.

3. Guiding principles

  • The best interests of children and vulnerable adults come first in every decision we make.
  • No one affiliated with the Foundation should ever use their position to exploit, abuse, or exert undue influence over a beneficiary.
  • Beneficiaries and their families have the right to understand what is being asked of them and to say no, including to being photographed or featured in our materials.
  • Concerns are taken seriously and acted on promptly, regardless of who raises them.

4. Photography, stories and consent

Where our Impact page notes that a photograph, video, or personal story is "coming soon" or is a placeholder, it is because we do not publish images or personal accounts of beneficiaries — particularly minors, widows, and patients — without appropriate consent. Where a child is involved, that consent is sought from a parent or guardian. We aim to describe what a photograph or video will show, and its date and location where known, rather than publish it before consent and verification are complete.

5. Reporting a concern

Anyone — a beneficiary, family member, staff member, volunteer, or member of the public — who has a concern about the conduct of anyone representing the Foundation, or about the welfare of a child or vulnerable adult connected to our programs, should contact us using the details on our Contact page. We will treat reports confidentially and will not require a reporter to confront the person concerned directly.

6. Staff and volunteer conduct

Everyone representing the Foundation at a medical camp, school visit, water-project commissioning, or community event is expected to treat beneficiaries with respect, avoid being alone with a child without another adult present, and follow the consent practices described above before taking or sharing any photograph or story.

7. Review of this policy

This is an initial draft, written to reflect the nature of the Foundation's work with women, widows, youth and students. It should be reviewed by a safeguarding specialist or legal counsel, and formally adopted by the Foundation's leadership, before publication.

8. Contact us

To raise a safeguarding concern, please use the Contact page. If a child or adult is in immediate danger, please contact local emergency services first.
